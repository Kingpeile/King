## 1. The Core Thesis — "Don't buy the king, buy what the king can't live without"

The central idea is a deliberate inversion of the obvious AI trade:

- **Don't buy NVIDIA.** NVIDIA is large, efficient, widely owned, and already priced for the AI buildout. There is no information edge there.
- **Buy the irreplaceable suppliers NVIDIA (and the hyperscalers) cannot do without** — the "chokepoint" / 瓶颈 / 卡脖子 nodes. These are tiny, obscure, often single- or few-supplier companies whose product the entire AI buildout physically must pass through.

The test he applies, paraphrased consistently across coverage **[2H]**: find the small company in the AI supply chain where **"if they stop supplying, the entire industry halts"** — companies institutions overlook and retail can't understand.

## 2. The METHOD — bottom-up supply-chain reverse-engineering

The method is **bottom-up reverse-engineering from a physical anchor**. Described consistently across the second-hand explainers and his own posts:

**Step 1 — Anchor on the physical artifact.**
Start from NVIDIA's **H100 / B200 / GB200 GPU supercomputing clusters** as the physical anchor — the thing the whole world is racing to build. **[2H, consistent]**

**Step 2 — Deconstruct layer by layer (BOM teardown).**
Tear the cluster apart down its bill-of-materials: GPU → optical interconnect / networking → optical modules / transceivers → the photonic engine → light source (laser) → modulator → the wafer/substrate the photonics are built on → the **raw material / feedstock** that substrate is grown from → the **equipment** (crucibles, MBE tools) needed to grow it. **[2H]** He "dissects BOMs and studies academic literature" to model the **"physical reality"** of the industry rather than market narrative. **[2H — singularityresearchfund Substack]**

**Step 3 — Keep going down until you hit the narrowest point.**
Continue deconstructing until you reach an **ultra-niche component or raw material that is physically irreplaceable and monopolized by a single or very few companies.** The deeper / more upstream, the better — the goal is the *bottleneck within the bottleneck* (e.g. AXTI is the InP-substrate bottleneck; InP itself is the deeper material bottleneck behind AXTI). **[2H + 1H]**

**Step 4 — Adversarial-AI stress test ("IC meeting").**
Before committing to a thesis, he **runs rigorous adversarial debates against multiple AI models** to surface flaws and counter-arguments — described as akin to a top-tier investment-committee (IC) meeting. He uses this to attack his own priors. **[2H — Odaily / PANews / KuCoin]** This is the part of the method most repeated as his "edge": by modeling the physics directly (BOM + academic literature + adversarial AI), his **priors are of higher quality than market consensus**, giving a probabilistic edge. **[2H]**

**Step 5 — Position AHEAD of the market, then let NVIDIA/institutions confirm.**
Buy before the market discovers the node. Then watch for **confirmation signals** — NVIDIA locking capacity, a hyperscaler design win, a CEO/analyst publicly naming the shortage — which trigger institutional rotation and re-rating. He explicitly treats "NVIDIA follows / NVIDIA confirms" as a validation heuristic. **[2H distillation]**

> **The riverbed metaphor [2H]:** "the narrowest point in the riverbed — no matter how high the flow, all the water has to squeeze through there." This is the visual definition of a chokepoint.

## 3. The Strait of Hormuz / geopolitical-chokepoint analogy

He borrows from **energy geopolitics**: ~20% of the world's crude oil passes through the **Strait of Hormuz**, making it a chokepoint where a small physical constriction controls a huge flow. **[1H/2H]**

> Applied: **"AXTI's relationship to AI optical module companies is similar to the Strait of Hormuz's relationship to global energy trade."** A single narrow node that the entire flow must transit, so whoever controls it has outsized leverage. He has also used the phrasing **"The Strait of AXTI."** **[2H, paraphrasing his posts]**

The point of the analogy: chokepoints are about **physical/geographic constriction**, not company quality. You don't have to be a great business to be un-bypassable — you just have to sit at the narrowest point.

## 4. Selection criteria (the filter)

A chokepoint candidate should score on **all** of these **[synthesized from 1H posts + 2H explainers]**:

1. **Irreplaceability / physical constraint.** The component can't be swapped out without redesigning the whole stack or breaking physics. Materials and substrates rank highest because they're bounded by chemistry/geology, not by engineering effort.
2. **Monopoly / concentration.** Single- or few-supplier control; he cites hard share numbers (e.g. InP "78% control by $AXTI"; "duopoly … 60%+ of the world's InP substrates"). **[1H]**
3. **"The market hasn't discovered it yet."** Underfollowed, no/low analyst coverage, retail "can't understand it," institutions "overlook it." The edge is *information asymmetry*, and it evaporates once the node is well-covered. **[2H]**
4. **Positioning AHEAD of the market.** Enter before the catalyst (capacity ramp, design win, public shortage admission). His self-description: he's been **"the first few to cover many supply chain photonic names."** **[1H — status/2032577275439485080]**
5. **Small market cap.** He fishes in sub-$1B and sometimes sub-$200M names where a re-rating can be a multiple, not a percentage. Examples he's cited as cheap: **$SIVE at ~$190–290M MC** ("grossly mispriced"); **$AXTI a ~$700M small cap**. **[1H]** (He does also hold/recommend larger names — TSEM, COHR, SOI — which he frames as "safest longs / defensible compounders," a different bucket from the asymmetric microcaps.) **[1H — status/2032577275439485080]**
6. **Geopolitical discount.** He explicitly accounts for geopolitical risk (e.g. China-controlled feedstock like gallium/indium) as both a *risk* and a *moat-deepener* for ex-China suppliers. **[2H distillation]**
