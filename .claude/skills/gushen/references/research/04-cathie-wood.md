## 1. Who she is (biography — facts)

- **Catherine Duddy Wood**, born **1955, Los Angeles**, eldest child of Irish immigrants; father a radar systems engineer (Irish Army → US Air Force). [2nd: Wikipedia, biographykind]
- **Notre Dame Academy** (all-girls Catholic HS), 1974 → **USC**, BS in finance & economics, summa cum laude, 1981. Mentored by economist **Arthur Laffer**. [2nd]
- **AllianceBernstein** 2001–2013, CIO of Global Thematic Strategies, managed >$5B. Earlier co-founded **Tupelo Capital Management** (hedge fund), and worked at Jennison Associates. [2nd]
- **Founded ARK Invest in 2014** after her proposal for *actively managed, transparent, innovation-focused ETFs* was **rejected at AllianceBernstein**. [2nd]
- **Devout Christian.** ARK is named after the **Ark of the Covenant**; her research team daily reading is *Jesus Calling*. Self-described "walk of faith." This faith framing is load-bearing for her conviction-through-drawdown temperament. [1st-ish: her own account quoted in Jesus Calling Magazine 2021 / 2nd]
- Flagship fund: **ARKK** (ARK Innovation ETF, launched Oct 2014). AUM peaked ~**$28B (early 2021)**, fund family peaked >$60B; collapsed to a fraction after 2021–22. [2nd: Morningstar]

## 2. Core mental models (deepest — the engine of her thinking)

### M1. Disruptive innovation is the *only* thing worth owning
- **1-line:** Only invest in companies on "the right side of change"; everything else is a value trap waiting to be disrupted.
- **Reasoning:** Technology platforms collapse the cost of doing things and disintermediate incumbents; the market's "safe" benchmark stocks are actually where the risk sits because disruption will hollow them out.
- **Examples:** (a) ARK runs *only* thematic innovation strategies — no diversified "core" product, by design. (b) Her framing: legacy indices = the danger zone, not the safe zone (see Quote Q1).
- **Sourcing:** [1st] ARK "Investors Beware: Stay on the Right Side of Change" article; [2nd] etftrends.

### M2. The 5 innovation platforms / 14 technologies (the map of the future)
- **1-line:** All real growth converges into 5 platforms — **Artificial Intelligence, Robotics, Energy Storage, Public Blockchains, Multiomic Sequencing** — spanning 14 underlying technologies.
- **Reasoning:** These platforms are simultaneously maturing and *converging* (e.g., AI + robotics + energy storage = autonomous mobility), producing super-exponential, compounding growth.
- **Examples:** (a) ARK's published thesis that disruptive innovation equity value grows ~40%/yr to **$200 trillion by 2030**. (b) 2025–26 "Great Acceleration" / "Golden Age" framing: 5 technologies reallocating a majority of global market cap. [1st: ARK Big Ideas; 2nd: Fortune, 36kr, kavout]

### M3. Wright's Law / cost-decline curves (莱特定律)
- **1-line:** For each cumulative doubling of units produced, cost falls by a fixed percentage — so model the *cost curve*, and demand/adoption follow.
- **Reasoning:** She prefers Wright's Law to Moore's Law because it's keyed to **cumulative production, not time**. Falling cost → exploding demand → more production → further falling cost (a flywheel). This is the quantitative core of every ARK price target.
- **Examples:** (a) EV battery & whole-car cost declines underpinning the Tesla thesis. (b) **DeepSeek (2025):** she framed it as *confirming* the thesis — AI inference/training costs falling **85–95% per year**, "just following that pattern," not a threat. (c) Robotaxi BOM falling toward **~$15,000**. [1st: her DeepSeek comments; 2nd: Morningstar, SCMP]

### M4. Innovation is **deflationary** — and deflation is the real macro story
- **1-line:** Technology drives prices *down*; the danger is deflation, not inflation; innovative, asset-light, deflation-adapted firms win.
- **Reasoning:** If costs are structurally collapsing, the firms that *create* the deflation capture the surplus, while debt-laden legacy businesses get crushed. This also makes her structurally pro-rate-cut / anti-tight-Fed.
- **Examples:** (a) 2023–24: "forget inflation, deflation is the real enemy... investors are worrying about the wrong thing"; said the Fed hiked "too far, too fast." (b) "Technology is deflationary... they know how to operate in a deflationary world." [1st: her CNBC/WSJ comments; 2nd: Fortune, Nasdaq, Benzinga]

### M5. 5-year minimum horizon — "truth wins out" through volatility
- **1-line:** Judge ARK over a rolling **5-year** window; over that horizon the truth about exponential technologies asserts itself regardless of short-term noise.
- **Reasoning:** Young, often-unprofitable disruptors need years to mature; short-term price = "uncertainty," not fundamental "risk." She explicitly reframes **volatility ≠ risk**. (Critics note this also makes the thesis hard to ever falsify — see §6.)
- **Examples:** (a) Repeatedly insists ARK should be measured on a 5-year basis. (b) "Volatility is more a function of uncertainty than risk per se." [1st: her interviews; 2nd: Insider Monkey, etfdb Q&A]

### M6. Being **wrong-footed short-term is a gift** — concentrate, and add on the way down
- **1-line:** Drawdowns put "innovation on sale"; if the thesis is intact, falling prices are a *buying signal*, and conviction means concentrating, not diversifying.
- **Reasoning:** If you believe the 5-year truth, a 70% drawdown in a thesis-intact name raises expected return. So she leans *into* declines and rotates toward her highest-conviction names during routs.
- **Examples:** (a) Jan 2022, mid-crash: declared **"innovation is on sale."** (b) Said critics shorting ARK are "shorting innovation." (c) Attributed a strong rebound to "concentrating on high-conviction stocks during the downturn." (This is also the mechanism behind the buy-high/sell-low *fund-flow* critique — §6.) [1st: her statements; 2nd: FT, ValueWalk, CNBC]

## 4. Famous decisions & track record (honest, including the bad years)

### The signature calls
- **Tesla — her defining mega-call.** Serial sky-high targets: famously **$4,000 split-adjusted** in the 2018 era, restated repeatedly; current standing target **~$2,600 by 2030** (≈90% of which she attributes to **robotaxi**). Tesla has been ARK's recurring top holding and the emblem of her brand. [1st: ARK models; 2nd: Motley Fool, Teslarati]
- **Bitcoin / crypto.** Long-running **$1M+** Bitcoin call; bull case stated as high as **$1.5M by 2030**, base case **$700K**; in 2025 *trimmed* the bull case ~$300K toward **$1.2M** citing stablecoin competition (a rare downward revision — note it). [2nd: TheDefiant, Yahoo/Motley Fool]
- **Coinbase (COIN)** — became ARKK's largest holding at points; core crypto-rail bet. [2nd: CNBC]
- **Roku, Zoom** — signature 2020 winners; emblematic of the COVID work-from-home / streaming surge that drove ARKK's boom. [2nd: Fortune, Morningstar]

### The arc
- **2020 boom:** ARKK returned **~+150%** in 2020; Wood became a retail-investor icon; AUM exploded (ARKK ~$28B peak early 2021, family >$60B). [2nd]
- **2021 turn:** ARKK **fell ~24% in 2021** even as the broad market rose. [2nd: Morningstar]
- **2022 crash:** ARKK **down ~67% in 2022** (vs Nasdaq ~ -33%) — i.e., roughly **double** the tech-index loss. Worst-performing US equity fund in Q1 2022 (-29.9%). **Peak-to-trough drawdown ~70–71%.** [2nd: Morningstar, Yahoo]
- **2024 verdict:** Morningstar branded the ARK family a **"value destroyer,"** estimating **~$14.3B of shareholder wealth destroyed 2014–2024** (ARKK alone ~$7.1B, #3 on its wealth-destruction list). From inception through Feb 2024, ARKK compounded ~**11.6%/yr vs ~15%/yr** for the average tech fund, with **higher volatility than nearly all peers** and **underperforming the S&P 500** — i.e., poor *risk-adjusted* returns. [2nd: Morningstar, TheStreet]
- **2025 recovery / AI-era narrative:** ARKK rebounded hard — **+23.4% through Q3 2025**, far ahead of S&P 500 (~+6.8%) — riding the AI/robotaxi/crypto narrative ("Golden Age," "Great Acceleration"). Still punctuated by sharp down quarters (e.g., a Tesla-driven weak quarter). The long-run risk-adjusted-vs-index question remains open. [2nd: TheStreet, kavout, stocktwits]

> **Net honest read:** spectacular timing-luck-and-thesis boom (2020) → catastrophic, double-the-index bust (2021–22) → strong but volatile rebound (2024–25). Investors who *bought the hype near the top* (most of the AUM arrived late) realized far worse returns than the fund's own time-weighted numbers — the classic behavior-gap.

## 6. Failures, criticisms, limitations (do NOT whitewash)

- **Catastrophic drawdown / value destruction.** ARKK -67% in 2022, ~70% peak-to-trough; Morningstar's "value destroyer," ~$14.3B family wealth destroyed 2014–24. Poor *risk-adjusted* returns vs S&P. [2nd: Morningstar]
- **Buy-high / sell-low fund-flow problem.** Most assets flowed in *after* the 2020 run; the typical ARKK investor's realized return is materially worse than the fund's stated return — the largest behavior gap among large funds. The very "transparency + hype" engine that grew AUM also delivered investors in at the worst time.
- **Thesis arguably unfalsifiable.** The "judge me over 5 years" + "volatility is uncertainty not risk" + "add on the way down" combination means *no* drawdown ever counts as disconfirmation; price drops are reframed as buying opportunities and price rises as vindication. Critics: the thesis can't lose on its own terms.
- **"One-trick pony" / perma-bull.** NPR and others: a single, always-bullish, long-duration-growth bet that works in zero-rate regimes and breaks when rates rise. No real risk management — "go-with-your-gut" risk approach worries Morningstar analysts; combined portfolio risk exposure is opaque.
- **"Marketer, not an investor."** Heavy critique that her edge is *distribution and narrative* (YouTube, podcasts, white papers, daily disclosure, TV) rather than security selection; ARK grew from ~$10M to >$40B substantially on her media presence. Stock-picking skill questioned (lagged peer tech funds).
- **Concentration & correlation risk.** Few names, huge weights, all the same macro/duration/liquidity factor — so the "diversified" thematic funds actually move together violently.
- **Forecasting talent.** Morningstar: compelling *map* of the future, weak *execution* — ARK "lacks the forecasting talent" to pick winners and manage the risks of such terrain.
- **Revisions.** The 2025 Bitcoin bull-case cut (~$1.5M → $1.2M) shows targets do move; bears say targets are marketing anchors, not falsifiable forecasts. [2nd]

## 7. Agreements vs disagreements with other 股神

### vs Warren Buffett & 段永平 — **sharp, foundational disagreement** (growth/vision vs value)
- **The core split:** Buffett/段永平 buy understandable businesses with durable moats and visible cash flows at a discount to intrinsic value, and *hold*; Wood buys pre-profit disruptors sized off TAM and cost curves, and *trades into volatility*. Buffett: Coke, Amex, financials, energy. Wood: Tesla, Coinbase, genomics, blockchain.
- **What Wood would say to them:** "You're anchored to today's cash flows and yesterday's moats — you're sitting in **value traps on the wrong side of change**; you systematically miss exponential, deflationary innovation because DCF on a falling-cost, exploding-TAM business looks 'expensive' until it isn't." (She'd note Buffett historically avoided/derided tech and famously called Bitcoin "rat poison.")
- **What they would say to her:** **Valuation and risk discipline matter; you ignore both.** Paying any price for a story is speculation, not investing; "volatility ≠ risk" is a rationalization for permanent capital loss; concentration in unprofitable, narrative-driven, rate-sensitive names with no margin of safety is how you destroy wealth (the 2022 result is Exhibit A). 段永平's "本分/不懂不做/便宜" framework is almost a point-by-point inversion of her method.
- **Subtle truce point:** both camps preach *long horizons* and *ignoring the crowd / benchmark* — they just disagree violently on what to hold and at what price.

### vs 葛卫东 (Ge Weidong) — **different domain, shared temperament**
- Different arena (葛卫东: commodity/futures trading then concentrated tech equity bets, esp. a famous large 浪潮信息 / AI-hardware-style position). **No direct philosophical overlap on method** (he's macro/trading-rooted, she's thematic-research-rooted).
- **Shared DNA:** outsized **high-conviction, concentrated big bets**, comfort with extreme volatility, and a willingness to look reckless to mainstream peers. Both are temperamentally "bet huge on a conviction and ride the swings" investors — contrast with the diversified-discipline of value investors.
